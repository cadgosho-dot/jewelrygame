# Gemology Reference Notes - v0.10.576

## Core reference framework

The professional loose-gem pages were edited against the following authoritative frameworks and professional principles:

- Gemological Institute of America: Gem Encyclopedia
- Gemological Institute of America: gemstone-specific Care and Cleaning Guides
- Gemological Institute of America: Gems & Gemology research on inclusions, synthetic growth and treatments
- CIBJO: Diamond Book, Gemstone Book, Pearl Book and Gemmological Laboratories Book
- Standard non-destructive gemological testing practice
- Advanced spectroscopy, fluorescence imaging and chemical analysis for cases that cannot be resolved by standard testing

For ivory compliance notes, the page directs users to current information from:

- Ministry of the Environment, Japan
- Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry, Japan
- CITES

## Editorial rules used in the game

- Mineral species, variety name, rock name, organic gem material, optical phenomenon and trade name are not treated as interchangeable.
- Identification, natural or synthetic origin, treatment status and geographic origin are treated as separate conclusions.
- Typical inclusions are presented as evidence that must be combined with other observations, not as automatic proof.
- Representative physical-property values may vary with composition, aggregate structure, treatment and measurement conditions.
- Mohs hardness is not used as a synonym for toughness, cleavage resistance, thermal stability or chemical stability.
- Geographic origin is described as an advanced laboratory opinion that requires comparison data and cannot be determined from color alone.
- Treatment disclosure and bench-care implications are kept separate from market-value judgments.
- The game does not replace an accredited laboratory report, legal advice or current official regulatory information.
